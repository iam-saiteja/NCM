EXP22: Is the emotional channel redundant with the state channel?
=================================================================

Question: e_emotional = L2_normalize(W_emo @ s_padded) with W_emo a
fixed orthonormal QR projection (ncm/encoder.py encode_emotional), so
the emotional channel is a deterministic linear function of the same
auto-state that the state channel compares. Does removing beta*d_emo
change retrieval quality?

Setup
- Seed: 20260819 (corpus order, held-out choices, query bootstrap); 20260820 for the conversation bootstrap
- Encoder backend: sentence-transformers
- Encoder state_dim: 7, emotional_dim: 3, AutoStateTracker dims: 5
- Corpus: experiments/data/real_world_corpus/train.jsonl (8939 records, counted at run time)
- Relevance label: a stored turn is relevant iff it shares the held-out query turn's session_id
- Label provenance: session_id ships with the corpus and was not authored for this experiment
- Query state: the 5-dim auto-state is inferred from the query text alone by a fresh AutoStateTracker and assigned to store.auto_state.state; no label information reaches the query, so no arm here is an oracle
- max_conversations: 100 (script default 100)
- Reproduce with: venv/Scripts/python.exe experiments/python/exp22_emo_ablation.py
- No synthetic or hand-authored data is used anywhere in this run.

Arms and how the removed weight was redistributed
- default: alpha 0.4, beta 0.2, gamma 0.3, delta 0.1
    redistribution: none; shipped defaults from ncm/profile.py RetrievalWeights
- no_emo_renorm: alpha 0.5, beta 0.0, gamma 0.375, delta 0.125
    redistribution: beta 0.2 removed, then alpha, gamma and delta each multiplied by 1/(1-0.2) = 1.25, giving 0.5, 0.375, 0.125; sum 1.0
- no_emo_state_absorbs: alpha 0.4, beta 0.0, gamma 0.5, delta 0.1
    redistribution: beta 0.2 removed and added entirely to gamma, giving gamma 0.5 with alpha and delta untouched; sum 1.0
- semantic_only: ncm.retrieval.retrieve_semantic_only, no weights
Total composite weight is 1.0 in every arm, so the ablation is not
confounded with a rescaling of the distance.

MDE definition: minimum detectable effect: the smallest true mean paired difference this design would detect with 80 percent power at a two-sided 0.05 level, computed as (z_0.975 + z_0.80) * sd_delta / sqrt(n_queries) = 2.801585 * sd_delta / sqrt(n_queries). A normal approximation to the paired t-test, quoted as a scale only. It ignores the conversation clustering, so it is optimistic
Wilcoxon: scipy.stats.wilcoxon, zero_method=wilcox, two-sided, method=exact. The exact null distribution assumes distinct absolute differences; the per-query metrics here are quantised so ties are present and no midrank correction is applied in exact mode. The exact p-value is the larger of the two and is reported for that reason. method=auto would have returned the normal approximation, which understates small-n p-values.

Structural note on how much the emotional channel can carry
- SentenceEncoder.state_dim is 7 and
  AutoStateTracker emits 5 dimensions, so 2 of the
  dimensions that encode_emotional pads to are structurally always
  zero, and the corresponding columns of W_emo never contribute.
- W_emo therefore maps an effectively 5-dim input to 3 dims.
- encode_emotional L2-normalizes its output, so e_emotional depends
  only on the direction of the auto-state. _rebuild_cache also
  L2-normalizes the stored auto-state before the state channel sees
  it. Both channels therefore read the same normalized quantity, one
  at 5 dims and one compressed to 3.
- Measured scale invariance of encode_emotional: max abs component
  difference between encode_emotional(s) and encode_emotional(s/||s||) over
  20 real inferred query states was 1.192e-07.

Reading of the result
The retrieval weights are proved live and beta is proved live at
its shipped 0.2, with a residual of
5.290e-08 on the identity
d_default - d_no_emo_renorm/1.25 - 0.2*d_emo, so the comparison
below is between genuinely different scorers and not between
copies.

Scale of the test: 228 queries drawn from 65 conversations.
Between the two redistributions and the three key metrics, the
number of queries whose metric actually changed ranges from
5 to 84 out of 228. Every paired
difference vector is therefore mostly exact zeros, and that has to
be read alongside any interval computed from it.

Removing the emotional term does not hurt retrieval quality on
this task. For both redistributions and all three key metrics,
zero lies strictly inside both the query-level and the
conversation-level 95 pct interval, so neither direction is
supported at that level. The point estimates weakly favour
removal: 6 of the 6 point estimates are
positive in the direction of removal, which is the direction a
redundant term would move if it were adding noise. That is a
sign pattern, not a detected effect.

What this does not license. It does not show that the emotional
channel earns none of its 0.2 weight, and it does not show that
the two arms are equivalent. A two-sided interval that contains
zero is not an equivalence test, and on difference vectors this
sparse it is close to uninformative about effect size. The
largest minimum detectable effect over these six cells is
0.0098 at 80 pct power and two-sided 0.05, so any true
effect smaller than that would have been missed by this design.

The ranking does move, so the arms are not identical
computations. The emotional term reorders results without
changing how many relevant turns land in the top k.

The semantic_only arm, which is the most consequential number
here, is not an ablation of the emotional term but a weight-free
external reference. Against the shipped composite it scores
P@5 +0.0079 with query-level CI [-0.0009, +0.0167] and
conversation-level CI [-0.0009, +0.0167], 22/228 queries changed.
NDCG@10 -0.0011 with query-level CI [-0.0050, +0.0028] and
conversation-level CI [-0.0043, +0.0022], 108/228 queries changed.
MRR +0.0072 with query-level CI [-0.0016, +0.0168] and
conversation-level CI [-0.0012, +0.0165], 16/228 queries changed.
Reading: on this corpus and this label, dropping the three
non-semantic channels entirely does not lose retrieval quality
either, and on P@5 the point estimate favours the semantic-only
retriever. That is a finding about the whole composite, not just
about beta, and it is the number a reader should be shown first.

The two channels are correlated at Pearson r = 0.4691 and Spearman
rho = 0.4767 over 9401 real (query, memory) pairs from
65 conversations. No p-value is quoted: the pairs are
clustered by query and by store, so an independence-based p-value
would be meaningless. The correlation is consistent with the
structural argument that both channels are deterministic
functions of the same normalized auto-state, so the emotional
term supplies no input the state term lacks. It is not an
algebraic identity, because the projection to 3 dimensions
distorts angles, so d_emo is not a function of d_state alone.

Mechanism. Pooled over all (query, memory) pairs, the emotional
distance has standard deviation 0.00427 against
0.15513 for the semantic distance, and after the
shipped weights that is 0.000853 against
0.062050. The channel is small in scale,
but it is not too small to act. Inside a single store its median
order-flip budget is 0.002570 against a median
adjacent-rank gap of 0.005709 in the top 12 of the
shipped composite, and
28.0 pct of those adjacent gaps (703 of
2508) are smaller than that query's
budget. So the term does reorder candidates, and the candidates it
can reorder are the ones the other three channels have already
placed within its reach. It reorders near-ties and carries no
relevance signal, which is why the ranking moves while the metrics
do not. It is not that the channel cannot separate candidates at
all.

This run measures beta only at its shipped 0.2. It says nothing
about what a larger beta would do.

Scope. One corpus, one relevance label, one query-state condition,
one encoder, one machine, and the shipped weight vector. This does
not show that an emotional channel is useless in general, and it
does not show that this one is worthless. It shows that on this
task, at this scale, removing this projection of this state does
not measurably change retrieval quality in either direction.

Latency is deliberately not measured here. exp4 is the latency
measurement. The composite path reads a cache warmed once per
conversation while retrieve_semantic_only rebuilds its own matrix on
every call, so timings taken here would compare cache states.
