nonzero is the count of targets whose metric actually changed,
out of the paired total. MDE is the smallest paired mean difference
this design would detect 80 percent of the time at two-sided 0.05.
A row whose nonzero count is small cannot produce an interval that
clears zero no matter what the effect is, so its CI carries no
effect-size information. ICC(1) above zero means targets inside a
conversation resemble each other and the target CI is too narrow. A
NEGATIVE ICC(1) means targets inside a conversation differ more than
targets drawn at random, which makes the design effect less than 1
and pushes the effective n above the nominal n. That is a real
property of these 14 conversations, not a gain in information, and
the cluster CI is then narrower than the target CI. Read the cluster
CI in both directions.

Per-channel decomposition across candidates
Ranking depends only on how much a channel VARIES across
candidates. A channel with near-zero std shifts every candidate by
nearly the same amount and rarely reorders them, whatever its weight.
'std share' is each channel's share of the SUM OF THE FOUR
PER-CHANNEL STANDARD DEVIATIONS. It is not a variance
decomposition: std(sum) does not equal sum(std) unless the channels
are perfectly correlated, and d_emo and d_state are correlated here.
A variance-based share would put d_sem higher still, so this share
understates d_sem's dominance rather than overstating it.

Structural zero padding
- AutoStateTracker emits 5 dims, encoder.state_dim is 7, encode_state returns 7 dims
- Trailing dims all zero: True, values [0.0, 0.0]
- AutoStateTracker emits 5 dimensions while SentenceEncoder.state_dim defaults to 7, so encode_state and encode_emotional zero-pad and dimensions 6 and 7 carry no information. Note that the composite state channel compares auto_state_snapshot directly at 5 dims, so the padding affects the emotional projection, not d_state.

Reading of the result
- Scale: 48 paired targets from 14 conversations, 200000 bootstrap resamples, hand-authored paraphrases.
- NCM P@5 changes by -0.0083 from verbatim to hand-authored paraphrase queries. Target-level 95pct CI [-0.0542, +0.0375]; conversation-level cluster CI [-0.0553, +0.0356], which is the one to read because the targets are clustered. The cluster CI includes zero, so no paraphrase regression is established at this sample size. 24 of 48 targets changed on this metric; the smallest difference this design would detect at 80 percent power is 0.0645.
- The semantic-only baseline P@5 changes by -0.0083 from verbatim to hand-authored paraphrase queries. Target-level 95pct CI [-0.0500, +0.0333]; conversation-level cluster CI [-0.0520, +0.0298], which is the one to read because the targets are clustered. The cluster CI includes zero, so no paraphrase regression is established at this sample size. 24 of 48 targets changed on this metric; the smallest difference this design would detect at 80 percent power is 0.0577.
- The targets are clustered by conversation, not independent. Measured ICC(1) for the NCM P@5 delta is +0.0069 over 14 conversations of mean size 3.43, a design effect of 1.017 and an effective sample size of 47.21 against 48 nominal observations.
- No difference in paraphrase P@5 loss between the composite and the bare encoder was detected: +0.0000, conversation-level cluster CI [-0.0213, +0.0255], zero strictly interior. At n=48 the smallest difference this design would detect at 80 percent power is 0.0289 P@5, so a composite-added fragility below that remains possible. 6 of 48 targets changed on this measure.
- MRR RISES under hand-authored paraphrase, by +0.0194 for NCM and +0.0002 for the semantic-only baseline. That is larger in magnitude than the largest P@5 movement (0.0083) and opposite in sign to it, so the word regression does not describe every metric in this table. The NCM MRR conversation-level cluster CI is [-0.0474, +0.0847], which includes zero, so the direction is not established.
- The two arms are NOT length-matched at this scale. The hand-authored paraphrases are longer by +5.6 characters on average (+5.6 percent), exact Wilcoxon on the paired character counts p = 0.0005. Query length is therefore a second manipulated variable alongside rewording, and this design cannot separate the two.
- No paired length-versus-metric Spearman correlation reaches p < 0.05 over the 6 metrics tested, the largest being semantic_only__mrr rho -0.0998 p 0.4996. No metric's per-target movement was found to track per-target length change. This does NOT clear the arm-level length difference reported above: a per-target rank correlation and a difference in arm means are different quantities, and with 48 targets this test is underpowered against a small one.
- Under verbatim queries, d_sem supplies 91.7 percent of the SUMMED per-channel across-candidate standard deviation; the shares are sem 91.7, emo 1.0, state 5.4, time 1.9 percent. This is a share of a sum of standard deviations, not a variance decomposition, because std(sum) does not equal sum(std) unless the channels are perfectly correlated. A variance-based share would put d_sem higher, so this figure understates its dominance.
- Under paraphrase queries, d_sem supplies 91.8 percent of the SUMMED per-channel across-candidate standard deviation; the shares are sem 91.8, emo 1.0, state 5.3, time 1.9 percent. This is a share of a sum of standard deviations, not a variance decomposition, because std(sum) does not equal sum(std) unless the channels are perfectly correlated. A variance-based share would put d_sem higher, so this figure understates its dominance.
- gamma*d_state has 18.3 times less across-candidate dispersion than alpha*d_sem (0.003240 against 0.059254), so despite carrying weight gamma=0.3 it rarely changes the order. It is not incapable of reordering: no single-channel ablation was run here, so the extra channels' contribution to the ranking is not attributed.
- retrieve_top_k_fast ignored its s_current_normalized argument: two opposite argument values produced an identical ranking.
- Assigning store.auto_state.state DID change the distances, so the state channel was genuinely driven and the low dispersion above is not a false negative from an uncontrolled input.
- Across targets within the verbatim arm alone, pairwise L2 between the 48 inferred query states spans 0.0052 to 0.0792 over 1128 pairs, against a per-dimension single-step ceiling of at most 0.125, because AutoStateTracker.update applies one EMA step from the neutral 0.5 vector.
- Across targets within the paraphrase arm alone, pairwise L2 between the 48 inferred query states spans 0.0032 to 0.0779 over 1128 pairs, against a per-dimension single-step ceiling of at most 0.125, because AutoStateTracker.update applies one EMA step from the neutral 0.5 vector.
- Within a target, the verbatim-to-paraphrase state shift is a separate and smaller quantity: mean L2 0.0128 over 48 targets. The earlier pooled figure mixed these two scopes.
- d_emo and d_state correlate at Pearson r = 0.2939 across candidates under verbatim queries. They are built from RELATED BUT DIFFERENT vectors, not the same one: e_emotional is a fixed linear projection W_emo of the PRE-update auto-state, while d_state compares the POST-update auto_state_snapshot of the same memory. Measured over 567 stored memories in 14 stores, 0 pairs are identical and the mean L2 gap is 0.017796, while the max L2 between the pre-update state at index i and the snapshot at index i-1 is 0.000e+00, which places them exactly one turn apart. r stays below 1 for two further reasons: W_emo has shape (3, 7), so it maps the 5 informative state dimensions into 3 and loses rank, and encode_emotional L2-normalizes the projection, which discards its magnitude (measured output norm 1.0000).
